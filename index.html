Abstract

Detecting AI-generated images is increasingly challenging as new generative models rapidly emerge. Existing detectors perform well on known sources but often fail to recognize images from unseen ones and cannot adapt without human supervision.

To address these challenges, we propose AS-AID, an Autonomous and Self-Adaptive AI-Generated Image Identification framework that autonomously detects AI-generated images and integrates models of novel forensic sources without human intervention. AS-AID unifies three key capabilities: (1) an AI-Generated Image Identification subsystem that categorizes an unlabeled image stream into real, known synthetic sources, and unknown synthetic sources using an enhanced forensic embedding space; (2) a New Source Discovery subsystem that isolates high-purity clusters representing newly emerging forensic sources among unattributed images; and (3) an Autonomous Adaptation and Validation subsystem that dynamically expands the embedding space to accommodate novel microstructures and validates each update without human oversight.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that AS-AID reliably discovers emerging AI sources, autonomously incorporates them into its model, and maintains high detection accuracy while existing detectors suffer substantial performance drop as new generators appear.

Key Capabilities of AS-AID

1. Identification Subsystem

Categorizes unlabeled image streams into real, known synthetic sources, and unknown synthetic sources via an enhanced forensic embedding space.

2. New Source Discovery

Isolates high-purity clusters representing newly emerging forensic sources among unattributed synthetic images without requiring external labels.

3. Autonomous Adaptation

Dynamically expands the embedding space to accommodate novel microstructures and validates updates without human oversight.
